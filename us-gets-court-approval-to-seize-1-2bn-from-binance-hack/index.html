US gets court approval to seize $1.2bn from Binance hack

The Justice Department says it has court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in crypto stolen from Binance, as recovery work continues.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 14, 2026 · 03:34 UTC#58e4652 min read

The US Justice Department has court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The action covers part of the funds frozen after last month’s hack, and recovery efforts are still underway, the department said Thursday.

The department’s announcement on the Binance theft said the US government and private sector have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets so far. The court-authorized amount is more than $1.2 billion of that total. The Associated Press reported the FBI’s seizure announcement on Thursday.

More than $6 billion was stolen

The theft happened on or about Oct. 29. The Justice Department says members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. The FBI had publicly attributed the attack to North Korea on Oct. 31.

The court action does not mean the government has recovered all the stolen crypto. The department said authorities and private companies worked with crypto service providers to freeze funds. It also said TraderTraitor actors continue to move assets through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. These services can help transfer or obscure the path of cryptocurrency.

Last week, the FBI, US Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation urged crypto firms to block transactions involving wallet addresses linked to the laundering of the Binance funds. The agencies also shared a list of addresses associated with the actors.

Recovery work continues

The Justice Department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are ongoing. Forfeiture is the legal process used to transfer property tied to crime to the government. The department said it intends to use its authority to seize and return North Korea’s gains, but its announcement did not set a date for any return of funds to Binance or its users.

The case now turns on tracing assets and pursuing them through legal proceedings. The department said the latest court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion, while the wider effort has frozen more than $2.7 billion. It did not provide a breakdown of the crypto assets or say how much of the remaining frozen funds may be subject to future court action.
