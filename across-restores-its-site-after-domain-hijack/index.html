Across restores its site after domain hijack

Across says it has recovered its domain and restored service after a phishing takeover, as it investigates the attack and promises to make affected users whole.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 11, 2026 · 19:02 UTC#7ebdf62 min read

Across Protocol has recovered its website and restored service after attackers hijacked its domain and sent visitors to a phishing page, putting some wallet users at risk. The cross-chain bridge said it would make affected users whole, but it has not disclosed how many people were affected. The Block’s report says the takeover began at about 18:45 UTC on Nov. 10.

How did attackers take over Across’s website?

Across attributed the takeover to social engineering targeting Namecheap, the company that manages its web address. In this kind of attack, someone tricks a service provider into giving them access. The attackers used that access to direct across.to visitors to a fake site.

Across said it was alerted within 15 minutes and began taking the site offline. Security response group SEAL911 flagged the domain as a phishing site with major wallet providers. Cloudflare marked it as risky and then removed its DNS records, which direct a web address to a server. That made the fake site inaccessible.

Could the fake site drain users’ wallets?

Yes. The phishing page tried to get visitors to sign token approvals or permits—permissions that can let another party move tokens from a wallet. The Block, citing security platform GoPlus, reported estimated losses of $350,000 across Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base, BNB Chain, Ink and Plasma. Across has not confirmed that estimate or disclosed a count of affected users.

Cointelegraph’s report says Across described the domain as back under its control and its systems as working normally. It also reported the team’s statement that third-party integrations and API users were unaffected.

What has Across promised affected users?

Across said it would make affected users whole and publish a full post-mortem in the coming days. A post-mortem is a report explaining what happened and how. The promise came as the protocol restored its site; the details of any payments and the cause of the registrar takeover remain unconfirmed in the reports.
