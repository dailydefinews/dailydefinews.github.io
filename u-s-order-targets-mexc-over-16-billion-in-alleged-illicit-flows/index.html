U.S. order targets MEXC over $16 billion in alleged illicit flows

A FinCEN order will bar U.S. institutions from sending funds to or from MEXC after Treasury alleged at least $16 billion in illicit flows over five years.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 9, 2026 · 16:37 UTC#8498082 min read

On Nov. 6, the U.S. Treasury’s Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) issued an order to block U.S. financial institutions from transferring funds to or from crypto exchange MEXC Global. The action could cut the exchange off from U.S. financial services. FinCEN’s announcement says the agency tied MEXC to at least $16 billion in transactions linked to sanctions evasion, cybercrime and other illicit activity.

When does the transfer ban take effect?

The order takes effect 30 days after its publication in the Federal Register, Treasury said. Until then, the ban is not yet in force. Once effective, it will prohibit U.S. financial institutions from processing transfers involving MEXC. The measure was issued under Section 9714 of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act.

Treasury says the case formally rests on Russian illicit finance, even though it announced the action as part of Operation Economic Outcast, a campaign targeting Iran’s access to global finance. CoinDesk’s report notes that Section 9714 applies to foreign institutions tied to Russian illicit finance.

What transactions did Treasury flag?

Treasury alleges MEXC handled the funds between May 2021 and May 2026. It says nearly $1.5 billion was linked to Russian sanctions evasion, including $232 million in direct transactions with entities sanctioned by the U.S., European Union or United Kingdom. Treasury also says MEXC processed transactions with Russian crypto exchange Garantex and its successor, Grinex, as recently as June 2026.

The agency further alleges MEXC sent or received about $1 billion in direct transactions with more than 40 Iranian crypto exchanges during that five-year period. It says about $22 million in transactions benefited Iran’s central bank and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, including its Qods Force.

Treasury described MEXC as one of the world’s 20 largest crypto exchanges. It said the platform processed more than $1 billion in daily transactions and held about $5 billion in reserves as of September 2026. Those figures and the alleged illicit flows are Treasury’s claims.

How has MEXC responded?

CoinDesk reported that MEXC said it was reviewing the order while continuing to operate normally. The exchange also said it had begun an independent review of its anti-money laundering and sanctions controls.
