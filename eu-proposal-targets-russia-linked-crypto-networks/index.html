EU proposal targets Russia-linked crypto networks

The European Commission has proposed new Russia sanctions covering crypto networks and 60 banks, with approval still needed from all 27 EU states.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 25, 2026 · 11:46 UTC#2cb54a2 min read

The European Commission has proposed targeting crypto and payment networks it says help Russia evade sanctions, as part of a wider package aimed at cutting Moscow off from funds linked to its war in Ukraine. The Associated Press report says the proposal also covers banks, energy and sporting events.

How would the proposal affect crypto networks?

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the package would crack down on crypto and payment networks in Russia and in third countries that help Russia evade existing sanctions. The Commission has also launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support that work, according to von der Leyen’s statement on the 22nd sanctions package.

The announcement does not name specific crypto platforms or countries. It also does not give details on how the task force will identify networks or enforce restrictions. The stated focus is on networks linked to sanctions evasion, rather than a general ban on crypto use.

What would happen to Russian banks?

The Commission proposes extending transaction bans to about 60 Russian banks and removing all 60 from SWIFT, the messaging system banks use to communicate about international payments. The measures are intended to make it harder for Russia to move money through the financial system.

Von der Leyen said the package would also widen restrictions on Russia’s energy sector. It would bar European companies from helping build, operate or repair Russian energy infrastructure, including refineries, and would cover electricity services for the first time. The proposal would list more than 70 ships linked to Russia’s shadow fleet, used to transport oil, and ban tanker vessels insured in Russia from EU ports.

Would Russian teams be barred from EU events?

The proposal would stop Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at sports events in the EU. It would also ban official Russian symbols at sports and cultural events, restrict Russian sponsors’ financial flows to European sports and cultural groups, and limit Russians from holding posts in European sports federations.

When could the sanctions take effect?

The package is a proposal, not a set of sanctions already in force. All 27 EU member countries must endorse it before it can take effect. The approval process may take weeks, and the measures could change during negotiations.
