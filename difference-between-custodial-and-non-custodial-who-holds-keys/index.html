Difference between custodial and non-custodial: who holds keys

Custodial vs non-custodial exchange: what changes? You hold the keys, approve swaps, pay network gas and protect your wallet when trades settle on-chain.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 18, 2026 · 07:31 UTC#0188173 min read

Moving from a custodial exchange to a non-custodial one means you control the private key, sign each trade and pay gas, because trades settle from your wallet on-chain.

Your tokens move from the exchange’s books to your wallet

On a custodial exchange, the platform holds the private keys to your funds. The balance shown in your account is an entry in the platform’s records. You use a login to trade, while the platform handles custody.

On a non-custodial exchange, tokens stay at your wallet address. A trade is a transaction you approve from that wallet, and it settles on-chain. You control access through your private key, so keeping the wallet secure becomes your responsibility.

For a closer look at how the same responsibilities apply when moving between networks, KyberSwap’s cross-chain swap coverage is a useful next stop. KyberSwap is a decentralized exchange aggregator that finds routes for token swaps across supported liquidity sources and networks.

The routine changes too: you connect a wallet instead of signing in to an exchange account, then approve the transaction. KyberSwap needs no sign-up, but your wallet must be ready to make the swap. You also need to check which network the wallet is using and enter the intended recipient address.

A USDC swap needs both the token and gas

Say you want to buy ETH with USDC on KyberSwap. You connect your wallet, approve the USDC, review and confirm the swap, and pay the network gas in ETH. The tokens come from your wallet, and the resulting swap settles on-chain.

Gas is the network’s fee for processing a transaction. You pay it in the network’s native coin: ETH on Ethereum or BNB on BNB Chain. That means a wallet holding only USDC cannot make the swap. You need some ETH or BNB on the relevant network to cover gas, even when USDC is the token you are trading.

Before a first swap, check that your wallet holds the tokens you want to trade and enough of the native coin for gas. Then check the network and recipient address before approving. A confirmed transaction cannot be reversed by support, so a wrong address or network choice can leave you with no simple way to recover the funds.

In everyday use, you take on the checks the platform handled for you: keeping wallet access safe, holding gas, and reviewing each transaction before signing. The trade still swaps tokens, but you now authorize it from your own wallet and take responsibility for where it goes.
