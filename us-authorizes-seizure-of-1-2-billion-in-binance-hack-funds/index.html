US authorizes seizure of $1.2 billion in Binance hack funds

US officials have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in Binance hack funds, part of a wider freeze as investigators trace the rest.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 15, 2026 · 17:52 UTC#8d8fbd2 min read

US officials have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday. The action is part of a wider effort to freeze funds from the exchange’s October hack, while investigators continue tracing the rest.

The Justice Department said the theft took place on or about Oct. 29. It attributed the theft of more than $6 billion in virtual assets to members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor. The FBI had previously attributed the attack to the group.

Since the theft, US agencies and private companies have worked with virtual asset service providers, firms that handle crypto transfers and storage, to freeze the stolen funds. The Justice Department said those efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion so far. The $1.2 billion covered by the court authorization is part of that total.

Tracing and forfeiture efforts continue

The court authorization allows the government to seize or restrain the assets. It does not mean the funds have already been returned to Binance or its users. As The Associated Press reported, the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Investigators are still tracking the assets. The Justice Department said TraderTraitor actors continue to move them through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchains; mixers obscure transaction trails. The FBI, the US Secret Service and the IRS issued an alert last week asking crypto firms to block transactions involving addresses linked to the stolen Binance funds.

The FBI’s Los Angeles field office, Cyber Division and Virtual Asset Unit are coordinating the recovery effort with the Secret Service and IRS. The Justice Department said the work to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets is ongoing. It did not give a timeline for the next court filings or for any return of funds.

The scale of the freeze is significant, but the latest announcement covers only part of the reported theft. The US government and private sector have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets, and the government has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of that amount, according to the Justice Department’s announcement.
