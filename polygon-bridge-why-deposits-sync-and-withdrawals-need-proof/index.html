Polygon bridge: why deposits sync and withdrawals need proof?

Polygon bridge state sync vs checkpoint explained: Ethereum deposits arrive through validator relays; Polygon withdrawals need a checkpoint and a claim.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 23, 2026 · 17:23 UTC#9296cd3 min read

Polygon bridge deposits use state sync, while withdrawals use checkpoints and a separate claim on Ethereum. The directions work differently because Polygon validators relay Ethereum events to Polygon PoS, while Ethereum reads Polygon activity through checkpoint proofs.

That difference explains why a deposit can arrive without a transaction from your address, but a withdrawal needs you to send another transaction. The Polygon PoS bridge is the route between Ethereum and Polygon PoS; the Polygon bridge link fits here for readers looking at that service: Polygon bridge.

How does state sync move a deposit to Polygon?

A deposit locks tokens on Ethereum, then Heimdall validators relay the event so Bor can mint the matching tokens on Polygon PoS.

  • You send one Ethereum transaction to deposit.
  • StateSender emits an event when the tokens are locked.
  • Heimdall validators relay that state sync event to Polygon.
  • Bor runs a system call to StateReceiver, which mints the tokens.

Heimdall coordinates validator activity and relays state sync events. Bor produces Polygon blocks and processes the message through StateReceiver, the Polygon contract that receives state sync data. The mint appears as a token transfer on Polygon.

Say you deposit tokens and then check your wallet. You may see the incoming token transfer without seeing a transaction sent from your address on Polygon. That is expected: Bor's system call handles the mint. Look for the token transfer, not a sent transaction from you.

For your costs, the deposit requires one Ethereum transaction. How long it takes can vary with Ethereum confirmation and the state sync relay and processing.

How do checkpoints release a Polygon withdrawal?

A withdrawal burns tokens on Polygon PoS, then a checkpoint commits Polygon activity to Ethereum so a proof can release the locked tokens.

  • You send a burn transaction on Polygon.
  • Heimdall validators produce a checkpoint with a Merkle root, a compact record of Polygon blocks.
  • More than two-thirds of validator stake must sign the checkpoint.
  • You submit proof of the burn on Ethereum to release the tokens.

Ethereum does not process every Polygon block directly. The checkpoint gives Ethereum a signed commitment to a set of Polygon blocks. The proof links your burn to that commitment, allowing the Ethereum side to release the corresponding tokens.

That means a withdrawal takes two transactions from you: the Polygon burn and the Ethereum claim. The claim also costs Ethereum gas. Until a checkpoint covers the burn and the proof is accepted, the release cannot complete.

What else should you know about Polygon bridge transfers?

How often are Polygon checkpoints submitted?

Heimdall validators submit checkpoints periodically, rather than for each individual withdrawal. The time a withdrawal takes depends on when a checkpoint covers its burn and when you submit the claim.

Why is my Polygon deposit taking longer than usual?

A deposit can take longer while the Ethereum transaction is confirmed or validators relay and Bor processes its state sync event. Confirm the Ethereum transaction and destination address, then check whether the token transfer has appeared on Polygon.

Is the Polygon bridge as secure as Ethereum?

Ethereum handles the lock and release, while Polygon validators relay deposits and sign checkpoints for withdrawals. The route depends on both the Ethereum contracts and Polygon’s validator process, so it does not rely on Ethereum alone.
