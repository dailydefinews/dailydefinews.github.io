Why Is My Bridged ETH Balance Smaller After Gas?

Amount received after bridging can be lower because Ethereum gas comes from your wallet balance, while route fees or swaps can change the amount delivered.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 14:40 UTC#04d0fe3 min read

After a bridge transfer, the wallet can show a bigger balance drop than the amount that arrives because Ethereum gas is paid from your ETH balance. The bridge amount and the wallet’s total balance change are different figures.

Suibridge locks ETH on Ethereum and mints the same asset amount on Sui. When you compare the two sides, use the deposit amount shown on Etherscan and the mint amount shown on Suiscan. For help with a separate wallet display issue, see Suibridge tokens missing from your wallet; Suibridge’s transfer record can help you tell a display problem from an amount difference.

Which amounts should you compare?

Compare the bridged deposit with the amount minted, then account for gas separately. Your wallet’s total balance change includes both the amount sent and the Ethereum transaction fee.

Say you send 1 ETH through Suibridge. Your Ethereum wallet spends 1 ETH for the transfer, plus gas paid in ETH. The Sui mint shows 1 ETH, so the wallet’s total drop on Ethereum is larger than the amount that arrived on Sui.

To trace those numbers, open the Ethereum transaction on Etherscan and find the amount deposited into the bridge. Then look for the corresponding Sui mint on Suiscan. The 1 ETH deposit is the amount to match against the 1 ETH mint; the gas belongs to the Ethereum transaction cost, not the bridged amount.

This comparison is useful after a completed transfer because it separates the bridge amount from the cost of sending it. A balance change alone combines them and can make the received amount look short.

When can the delivered amount really be lower?

A route that includes an aggregator or swap can deliver less of the expected output token because route fees and slippage affect the swap. Slippage is the difference between an expected swap rate and the rate the trade gets. In that case, compare the token amount sent with the final swap output, rather than treating the transaction’s total wallet change as the bridge amount.

The token can also change during the route. You might send ETH on Ethereum and receive SUI on Sui. Those are different assets, so their displayed quantities are not a like-for-like amount comparison. Check which token the route was set to deliver before deciding that value is missing.

For a direct Suibridge transfer of the same supported asset, match the Ethereum deposit amount to the Sui mint amount. For a route with a swap, account for its fees and output token. Add Ethereum gas to explain the wallet’s total balance drop.
