How to fund a Mantle NFT mint

To mint an NFT on Mantle, move the right token to the network, keep MNT for fees and check that the funds arrived before you connect to the mint.

Onchain Report NewsroomOct 16, 2026 · 19:13 UTC#4cbf883 min read

To mint an NFT on Mantle, bridge funds to the network, keep some MNT for transaction fees and check your balance before the sale begins. Mantle is an Ethereum layer 2, a network that processes transactions alongside Ethereum. Your wallet may show the same address on both networks, but the balances are separate.

What do you need before bridging to Mantle?

First, check the mint page to see which token it accepts and which network it uses. Many mints ask for ETH, but the project’s instructions are the place to confirm. You will also need a wallet with funds on the source network and enough of that network’s native token to pay for the bridge transaction.

A bridge moves assets between networks. For a fuller explanation of how deposits work and how to check they arrived, read this guide to the mantle bridge. Before you start, check the token and destination network shown in the bridge. A token with the same name can exist on more than one network.

How do you bridge funds to Mantle?

Connect your wallet to the bridge, choose the source network and Mantle as the destination, then select the token and amount. Review the transaction details before approving it in your wallet. The bridge may ask you to approve a token first. That approval gives the bridge permission to move that token up to the amount you approve; it is separate from the deposit itself.

After the deposit is confirmed on the source network, the bridge processes the transfer and credits the destination balance. Timing can vary with network conditions and the bridge’s process. Keep the transaction record so you can check its status if the balance takes time to appear.

How do you check the funds are ready to mint?

Switch your wallet to Mantle and check that the expected token appears in the balance. A wallet can be on the right network while showing no funds because the transfer is still processing, or because it is displaying a different token. Use the transaction details to confirm the destination and amount.

Before minting, make sure you have MNT available for Mantle transaction fees. The mint payment and the fee are separate costs: holding enough of the token the project accepts does not guarantee you can submit the transaction. Once the funds and fee balance are visible, connect your wallet to the mint page and check the network prompt before signing.

A short pre-mint check can prevent a rushed mistake:

  • Confirm the mint’s network and payment token.
  • Check the bridge’s destination is Mantle.
  • Verify the funds arrived in your Mantle wallet.
  • Keep MNT for transaction fees.

For most readers, the simplest route is to bridge only what the mint requires, plus a small buffer for fees. Check the received balance before connecting to the mint. That gives you time to resolve a delay or token mismatch before a sale starts.
