Why a gas quote can change during a transfer

A gas quote is an estimate or cap, while the network price can move before a transaction is included. Know what your wallet fixes before you confirm.

Onchain Report NewsroomOct 17, 2026 · 04:12 UTC#60bd423 min read

A fixed gas quote can differ from the live gas price by the time a transfer reaches the network, because the quote is made before inclusion and network demand can change. The key is to check what the wallet fixes: an estimated cost, a fee cap, or the exact fee. Those are different promises.

This matters when a transfer has several steps, such as a move between blockchains. The first transaction may wait before it is included, while a service displays a refreshed estimate for the next step. For background on a bridge that is no longer operating, read RenBridge’s history and what users can do. The same fee basics apply whether a transfer uses a bridge or a single network.

What does a fixed gas quote actually fix?

A fixed quote fixes only what the wallet or service says it fixes. Often, it is a snapshot of the estimated network fee at that moment, shown so you can decide whether to proceed. It does not make the network hold that price for you.

On Ethereum, a transaction sets a gas limit, which is the most gas it can use, and fee fields that set what you are willing to pay per unit. With a typical fee-cap transaction, the actual price depends on the base fee—the network’s required rate in the block—and your tip, up to your maximum. You pay for gas used, not automatically the full gas limit.

A service could instead promise to cover any difference above its quote. That would be a separate policy, not a feature of the blockchain. Look for clear wording about who pays if the live fee rises.

Why can the gas price move mid-transfer?

Network demand changes from block to block. If more transactions compete for space while you review a quote or wait for inclusion, the base fee or the tip needed to be competitive can rise. A displayed estimate may update, even though the amount you first saw looked fixed.

Once you sign and broadcast a transaction, its fee fields are part of that transaction. The wallet cannot silently alter them. If the fee cap is too low for current conditions, validators may leave it pending until conditions change, or you replace it with a new transaction that has a higher fee.

Some transfers also involve a later transaction on another network. Its fee is a separate charge, estimated under that network’s conditions. A quote for one step does not necessarily lock in fees for every later step.

What should you check before confirming?

Read the confirmation screen for the fee currency, maximum fee, and whether the quote expires. Check whether the screen is asking you to sign now or just showing an estimate for a later step. If a refreshed quote appears, compare the new total before proceeding.

  • Separate the gas limit from the fee price. The limit caps gas use; it is not the amount charged by itself.
  • Check the maximum fee and the asset used to pay it.
  • For a multi-step transfer, review each network fee when that step is ready.

For most readers, the wallet’s current suggested fee is the simplest choice when timing matters. If waiting is acceptable, a lower fee may save money, but the transaction can take longer or remain pending. Treat a fixed quote as a reliable price only when the service clearly says it will cover any increase.
