Is Aibai right for your app’s crypto transfers?

Aibai may fit a crypto transfer flow, but only if its supported assets, networks, custody model and failure handling match what your app needs to serve real users.

Onchain Report NewsroomOct 7, 2026 · 09:33 UTC#ba42743 min read

Aibai is right for your app only if it supports the assets and networks your users need, and gives your team a clear way to handle transfers that fail. That sounds simple, but “send crypto” can mean very different things. A user may be moving funds between wallets, paying another person, or withdrawing from an app. Each flow has different needs.

Before choosing a provider, map the journey from the user’s balance to the recipient’s wallet. Check where assets are held, who starts the transfer, what fees appear, and how the app reports progress. The aibai transfer approach is one point to assess against those requirements. A name or a polished send screen alone cannot show whether the underlying flow fits.

What should you check about aibai before adding it?

Check that the transfer works for the exact asset, network and direction your app supports. A token can exist on more than one network, and the sender and recipient must use a compatible one. If they do not, funds may not arrive as expected.

Ask for a clear list of supported assets and networks, plus the rules for deposits and withdrawals. Then establish whether your app or a third party holds the keys. In a custodial setup, a provider holds or controls them. In a non-custodial setup, the user controls them. That choice affects recovery, permissions and the work your team must do.

How do crypto transfers work inside an app?

A transfer usually moves through a few steps: the app checks the destination, shows the amount and fee, asks the user to approve, then sends a transaction to the network. The network records it in a public ledger. The app must then update the user when the transfer is pending, confirmed or failed.

That last step matters. A screen that says “sent” too early can mislead users when a transaction is still waiting for confirmation. Your app also needs a way to show a transaction reference, so support staff and users can check what happened.

  • Coverage: Can users send and receive the assets and networks they actually need?
  • Costs: Are network fees clear before approval, and can they change before the transfer is processed?
  • Control: Who holds the keys, and what happens if a user loses access?
  • Visibility: Can the app show pending, completed and failed states accurately?

Is aibai a good fit for every crypto app?

No transfer setup fits every app. A wallet that mainly lets users move funds needs dependable network coverage and clear status updates. An app that pays many recipients may also need batch handling and tools to reconcile payments. The right choice depends on the job, not the number of features on a product page.

Before committing, test a small transfer on each network you plan to support. Confirm the fee, recipient address format, confirmation time and failure path. If aibai can meet those needs and explain who controls funds at each step, it may suit the flow. If key details are missing, keep the integration out of production until you can verify them.
