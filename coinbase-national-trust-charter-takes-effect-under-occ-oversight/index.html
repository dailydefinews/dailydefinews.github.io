Coinbase National Trust charter takes effect under OCC oversight

Coinbase National Trust Company’s OCC charter took effect Nov. 6, putting its custody work under federal oversight while leaving it outside full-service banking.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 10, 2026 · 18:16 UTC#530e722 min read

Coinbase’s national trust charter took effect on Nov. 6, bringing its custody business under federal oversight by the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC), the U.S. bank regulator. The charter gives Coinbase National Trust Company permission to provide trust services; it does not make the company a full-service bank.

The OCC’s application record lists the charter as approved on April 2 and effective on Nov. 6. Coinbase announced the final approval on Nov. 9, saying the charter puts its custody services under a federal regulatory framework.

What services does the charter cover?

It covers digital asset custody, which means holding customers’ crypto for safekeeping. The OCC’s charter decision also allows the trust company to help customers move assets into and out of custody accounts. It can connect custody customers with certain Coinbase affiliate services, including staking and financing tied to assets held in custody.

The OCC approved the company’s plan to use fiduciary powers, which are duties to act in the interests of customers whose assets it holds. The bank regulator said Coinbase National Trust must stick to trust company business and related activities under its approved plan. It also said the company did not propose to issue stablecoins through the trust company.

Does this let Coinbase operate like a regular bank?

No. The charter does not authorize ordinary banking services such as retail deposit accounts or commercial lending. The OCC decision says the company will not be an insured depository institution, so customer deposits there will not have FDIC insurance.

That distinction matters because a national trust charter sets federal rules for the company’s custody and related services without giving it the wider powers of a commercial bank. Coinbase said the new framework builds on its existing work with New York’s financial regulator, which approved a limited-purpose trust charter for Coinbase Custody Trust Company in 2018.

When did the approval become final?

The OCC approved Coinbase’s application conditionally in April, then recorded the charter as effective on Nov. 6. Coinbase’s Nov. 9 announcement described the OCC’s sign-off as final approval. The regulator’s public record names the institution Coinbase National Trust Company and lists its headquarters in New York City.
