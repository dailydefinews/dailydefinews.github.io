Binance says fake job interview helped enable $6.34bn hack

Binance says a fake job interview helped hackers reach its wallet systems before $6.34bn in crypto was taken, exposing a months-long breach of exchange security.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 8, 2026 · 11:56 UTC#efa15d3 min read

Binance says hackers used a fake job interview to get into its systems before taking $6.34 billion in crypto from the exchange’s wallets on Oct. 29. The account shows how a recruitment scam became part of a months-long attack on the security systems that protect customer assets.

In its initial incident report, Binance said the attack combined malicious software, compromised devices and an internal AI tool. The company said $2.85 billion of the affected assets had been frozen or intercepted. It also said it would cover the losses and had restored 98% of affected tokens as of Oct. 31.

How did the fake job interview get inside Binance?

In September, an engineer was contacted on a legitimate job-search platform by someone posing as a project director. Binance said the engineer had listed their Binance experience on the platform. The supposed recruiter arranged two interviews: first with a fake human resources representative on video, then with someone posing as the head of a fictitious company.

During the second interview, the engineer was asked to download a technical test to a work computer. Binance said the test contained malicious code that ran during setup and went undetected by the company’s device security tools. The attackers used that access, along with an internal AI tool, to reach two wallet engineers’ computers on Oct. 20.

The job scam was one part of the attack. Binance said an earlier incident in June began when another engineer downloaded a modified Homebrew installer while looking for an AI coding tool. The company detected the problem and replaced the device, but said the attackers had exposed the engineer and colleagues working on wallet systems. Binance described the operation as a multi-stage campaign, rather than a single breach.

How did attackers move funds from the wallets?

On Oct. 29, attackers used stolen credentials to access an engineer’s computer, deploy a container and run a malicious script, according to Binance. Funds began moving at 20:05 UTC. Five Binance hot wallets—wallets connected to the internet—sent 233.46 million XRP to an attacker-controlled address in four minutes. Binance’s report also lists transfers of SOL, OP, BTC and ETH.

The attackers exploited Ethereum’s EIP-7702 feature to give a smart contract partial control over a hot wallet. Binance said the authorizations let transfers continue outside its usual withdrawal system, and that its multi-party signing setup made them harder to revoke quickly. The final unauthorized transfer was confirmed at 23:56 UTC.

Binance CEO Richard Teng called it a campaign built over many months, according to CoinDesk’s report on the exchange’s briefing. Binance said the FBI attributed the attack to North Korea. The exchange’s investigation is ongoing, and it said independent security experts will review its findings.
