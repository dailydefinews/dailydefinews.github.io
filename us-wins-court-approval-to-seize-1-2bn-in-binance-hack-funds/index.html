US wins court approval to seize $1.2bn in Binance hack funds

The Justice Department says courts have authorized action against more than $1.2 billion in Binance hack funds, as investigators and crypto firms keep tracing the rest.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 14, 2026 · 19:13 UTC#741a272 min read

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday, November 12. The Justice Department announcement says the effort is part of a broader push to stop North Korean-linked hackers from moving the funds.

The theft happened on or about October 29. The department says members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. The FBI previously described the incident as a major crypto heist. The Associated Press report says the FBI attributes the attack to the group.

The $1.2 billion is part of a larger pool of funds already frozen. US agencies and private companies have frozen more than $2.7 billion so far, according to the Justice Department. The court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of that amount. A freeze blocks assets from being moved; seizure or restraint gives authorities legal power to hold them while the case proceeds.

Investigators are working with virtual asset service providers, companies that handle crypto transfers and storage, to track and block the money. The Justice Department says the hackers are still laundering assets through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move tokens between blockchains, while mixers make the path of funds harder to trace.

The recovery effort is still underway. The FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are coordinating the work, and the Justice Department says authorities are continuing to trace, freeze, seize and seek forfeiture of the assets. Forfeiture is the legal process used to transfer property to the government.

That process may determine who can claim recovered funds. The AP reports that US authorities typically file a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then considers whether anyone has a legitimate claim to the assets. The Justice Department has not said how much of the stolen crypto may ultimately be returned to Binance or others.
