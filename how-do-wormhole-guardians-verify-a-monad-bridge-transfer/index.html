How Do Wormhole Guardians Verify a Monad Bridge Transfer?

How do Wormhole Guardians verify my bridge transfer? They wait for Ethereum finality, collect 13 of 19 signatures, then Monad checks the VAA before release.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 20, 2026 · 10:14 UTC#76e5cb3 min read

Wormhole Guardians verify a transfer to Monad by watching the Ethereum message, waiting for its required finality, and signing it; Monad then checks the signatures because it cannot read Ethereum directly. A supermajority of 13 out of 19 Guardians must sign before the destination can release funds.

What happens after I send 0.5 ETH?

The bridge contract locks the 0.5 ETH and publishes a Wormhole message with a sequence number and payload. The payload carries the transfer details, including where the funds should go.

Each Guardian watches Wormhole’s core contract on Ethereum for the LogMessagePublished event. That event tells the network a message was published, but Guardians wait for the source chain’s finality level, which is set in the message, before signing.

After that wait, each Guardian independently signs the message hash and gossips its signature to the others. Once 13 of the 19 Guardians have signed, their signatures can be assembled into a VAA, a signed attestation that the Ethereum message happened.

For someone moving assets from Ethereum, a Monad bridge means the Ethereum transaction starts a second step: Wormhole must attest to it before Monad can act. The bridge transfer is not complete just because the source transaction appears in your wallet.

What does Monad check before releasing the funds?

Monad’s Wormhole core contract checks the VAA signatures against the current Guardian set and the set’s index. If the signatures pass, the bridge contract checks that the message came from the expected emitter, then releases or mints the destination asset for the recipient.

Guardians attest that the message was published after Ethereum reached the required finality. They do not choose the amount: the payload created by the source transfer fixes the transfer details that the destination bridge processes.

That distinction explains the trust step. Monad cannot inspect Ethereum’s transaction history itself, so its contract relies on the supermajority’s signatures to confirm the source event. The Monad bridge therefore waits for both a valid attestation and the bridge’s own message checks before funds are released.

The longest wait is often on Ethereum: Guardians do not sign until the message’s required finality level is reached. Then signatures must accumulate, and a relayer must deliver the VAA to Monad. If the VAA is ready but the relayer never submits it, you can fetch it from Wormholescan and submit it on Monad yourself; anyone can do this. That can move the transfer forward without waiting for that relayer.

Related

What is a VAA in Wormhole?

A VAA is the signed message carrying the Guardians’ attestation. For this transfer, it lets Monad’s core contract check that enough Guardians signed the Ethereum message.

Can Wormhole Guardians steal bridged funds?

Guardians sign the message hash, while the payload fixes the amount and destination details. Monad also checks the signatures and expected emitter before the bridge releases or mints funds.

For a pending transfer, look up its message on Wormholescan and see whether the VAA is available. If it is, submitting it on Monad can help you complete the transfer and confirm the funds have reached the destination.
