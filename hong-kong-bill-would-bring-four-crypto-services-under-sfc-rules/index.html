Hong Kong bill would bring four crypto services under SFC rules

Hong Kong has gazetted a bill to bring crypto dealing, advice, management and custody under SFC oversight, with lawmakers set to consider it on Dec. 2.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 21, 2026 · 04:06 UTC#e9cac62 min read

Hong Kong has published a bill that would bring crypto dealers, advisers, asset managers and custodians under securities regulator oversight. The proposal could mean new authorization rules for businesses that handle crypto trades, advice, portfolios or safekeeping. The Hong Kong government announcement says the bill was gazetted on Nov. 20.

The bill proposes changes to the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance. Under the plan, businesses providing any of four virtual asset services in Hong Kong would need a license from, or registration with, the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC). They would also face a “fit-and-proper” test, a check on whether they are suitable to provide the service.

  • Dealing: Firms that handle virtual asset transactions for clients would need SFC authorization.
  • Advisory: Businesses giving clients advice on virtual assets would be covered.
  • Management: Firms managing virtual asset investments would need to meet the proposed rules.
  • Custody: Businesses safeguarding clients’ virtual assets would also come under the regime.

The government has not set out in its announcement the detailed boundaries for each activity. The proposed rules would align with standards used for conventional financial services in Hong Kong, according to the official bill announcement.

The change would also reach some financial firms already supervised by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority. Banks and stored-value facility licensees would have to register with the SFC if they want to provide virtual asset services. The bill would give both regulators powers to carry out the regime.

For crypto users, the proposal could bring more of the businesses around trading and holding digital assets into a formal regulatory system. But the announcement does not say when the licensing rules would take effect or set out a transition period for firms.

The bill is not yet law. It is due for its first reading in Hong Kong’s Legislative Council on Dec. 2. A first reading is an early step in the legislative process, so the proposal may still change before it is enacted.
