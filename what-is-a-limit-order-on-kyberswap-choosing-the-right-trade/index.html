What Is a Limit Order on KyberSwap? Choosing the Right Trade

What is a limit order on KyberSwap? It waits for your chosen price and may never fill; choose a regular swap when you need the trade done now.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 16, 2026 · 19:17 UTC#a1cbfd3 min read

KyberSwap Limit Order waits for a chosen price, while a regular swap trades now at the available market price. A limit order is a signed offer from a maker that a taker can accept; KyberSwap Docs explain its price conditions, expiry and cancellation. The order is signed off-chain, so placing it costs no gas. It fills only when the market reaches the set price and a taker accepts it. An expiry time ends an unfilled order.

How a KyberSwap limit order compares with a swap

A limit order lets you name the price you want and wait; a swap asks the KyberSwap Aggregator to find a route through supported liquidity sources for a trade now. The choice comes down to urgency, target price and how certain you need the fill to be.

Say you and a friend each want to sell the same amount of ETH for USDT, and the market price is the same for both. You want to sell only above today’s price, so you set a limit order at your target and wait. Your friend needs to sell now, so they use a regular swap. The shared trade and market conditions are unchanged; the target price is what changes the result. You may get your chosen price, but the order can remain open without filling. Your friend gets a trade now at the available market price.

Use a swap when the trade must happen now. Choose a limit order when a particular price matters more than timing, and you can accept that it may never fill.

With a limit order, the tokens stay in your wallet while the order waits, but the approval remains live. Approval gives the contract permission to use the token for settlement. That permission is separate from the signed order: cancelling the order does not cancel the allowance. Treat them as two tasks. Cancel an order you no longer want, then separately review and revoke its token approval if you do not want that permission to remain. An order can also expire, so keep track of open orders and their expiry.

That matters if you forget an order is open and spend the same token elsewhere. The balance may no longer cover what the order offers, which can affect whether it fills. Keep a note of open orders and check them before using those tokens for another trade. To place or manage a trade, KyberSwap is the place to compare the two options; for a separate next step after choosing, KyberDAO staking is a way to turn to KyberDAO participation.

How to cancel a KyberSwap limit order

Cancel an open order when you no longer want a taker to accept its price. Cancellation ends the order; review the token approval separately because it can remain live after the order is cancelled.

Can a limit order fill partially

Yes. A taker can fill only part of an order, leaving the rest open until it fills, is cancelled or reaches expiry. Check the remaining amount before treating the trade as complete.
