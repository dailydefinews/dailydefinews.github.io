Tether wins 15 backers for Bitcoin mining software

Fifteen mining firms will build integrations for Tether’s open-source MDK, aiming to let software work across different Bitcoin mining systems.

Onchain Report NewsroomOct 25, 2026 · 04:12 UTC#478ff02 min read

Tether says 15 Bitcoin mining operators, hardware makers and mining pools have committed to build and maintain integrations for its open-source Mining Development Kit (MDK), a move aimed at helping software work across different mining systems. The company announced the commitments on October 24 in its MDK industry backing announcement.

The participants include mining companies Bitdeer and Foundry Digital, chip makers BITMAIN and MicroBT, and pools F2Pool and OCEAN. Tether says the group will also include Canaan, Luxor Technology, Proto, EMCD, SpiderPool, Stratum V2, Heatbit, Elektron Energy and ACME Swisstech.

How will MDK connect different mining systems?

Each participant plans to build an MDK Worker, a software layer that translates its existing system into a format that applications using MDK can understand. ASICs, the specialized chips used to mine Bitcoin, pools and other systems can then connect through the same open framework.

Mining sites may run thousands of machines from different makers, connected to different pools and managed with separate software. That can mean building a new custom link for each system when adding an application. Tether says MDK is intended to let developers build against one common interface instead.

Companies choose which functions their Workers expose, according to Tether. They do not need to replace their existing technology or hand over control of their systems. If more participants build Workers, an application could work across more supported equipment and services.

What could the shared layer let operators automate?

Tether says a common interface could let monitoring tools and AI agents check machines, spot problems and optimize performance across different systems. The company’s QVAC mining agent is already operating directly on MDK, which Tether describes as an early example of an AI application using the shared layer.

The announcement describes that as a demonstration of the approach, not a report of a system already managing the entire industry. The 15 commitments are an initial step, and Tether says the framework remains open to other manufacturers, pools, operators and developers.

When will MDK be ready?

MDK is open source now, so developers can begin testing integrations and building applications, Tether says. The company is targeting its first stable 1.0 release by the end of 2026. That timeline gives participants time to develop their Workers, while the broader promise depends on more systems becoming compatible with the framework.
