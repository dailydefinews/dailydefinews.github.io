Small Token Balances to ETH: A One-Chain Sweep

How to convert small token balances into ETH: swap only tokens worth more than approval and gas, and choose native ETH to avoid an extra unwrap.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 18, 2026 · 15:19 UTC#d135993 min read

A one-chain sweep can turn leftover tokens into native ETH while leaving balances too small to recover untouched. The practical rule is to swap only tokens worth clearly more than their approval and swap gas. On Ethereum, gas can outweigh what a small balance returns; Arbitrum and Base usually make smaller swaps more worthwhile. The KyberSwap Aggregator finds routes across supported liquidity sources. For background on how KyberSwap routes swaps, see kyberswap classic.

How can you convert small token balances into ETH?

Start with your wallet connected to KyberSwap on the network where the tokens already sit, and keep some ETH there for gas. ETH is the native gas token on Ethereum, Arbitrum and Base, so a swap on that chain can pay gas without bridging your tokens elsewhere.

  1. List the balances and their values. Note each token and its approximate value on that chain. Treat the list as a cleanup queue, not a target to empty at any cost: a balance with little value may cost more to move than it can return.
  2. Drop tokens worth less than the gas. For each balance, compare its value with the wallet’s approval and swap gas estimates. Keep it only when the value is clearly higher than both costs. Gas estimates can change, so check them for the transaction you are about to make. Smaller balances can make more sense on Arbitrum or Base, where gas is far lower than on Ethereum.
  3. Select a token and choose native ETH as output. Set the received asset to ETH on the same network. Do not choose WETH, the wrapped form of ETH, if your goal is native ETH in the wallet. WETH needs a separate unwrap transaction to become native ETH, adding another step and gas cost.
  4. Compare the minimum received with gas. Review the minimum amount the swap says you will receive, then compare it with the approval and swap gas shown in the wallet. Skip the trade when the costs approach or exceed that amount. Interacting with an unknown airdropped token can also trigger a malicious approval, so leave unfamiliar airdrops alone.
  5. Approve the exact amount, then swap. An ERC-20 token needs approval before it can be swapped; native ETH needs no approval. Approve only the amount you plan to trade. If you will not swap that token again, revoke its approval afterward using your wallet or a trusted approval tool; revoking also requires a transaction and gas.
  6. Confirm the sweep worked. Repeat for the other balances that passed the cost check. The job is done when the wallet’s ETH balance has risen and the selected token balances read zero. Illustratively, a wallet that started with several small tokens on Base still holds them on Base afterward only if they were skipped; successful swaps change those token balances into native ETH, while the network and wallet stay the same. Save the gas estimates you see as a quick filter for the next cleanup, so you avoid rechecking tokens that clearly cost too much to recover.

Related questions

What should be done with tokens worth less than the gas fee?

Leave them in the wallet until their value is clearly above approval and swap costs. A zeroed token balance is not worth paying more than it returns.

Is it safe to sell airdropped tokens I don't recognize?

Skip unknown airdropped tokens. A swap may require an approval, and interacting with a malicious token can put the wallet at risk.
