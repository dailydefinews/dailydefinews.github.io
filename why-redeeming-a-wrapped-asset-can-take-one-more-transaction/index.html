Why redeeming a wrapped asset can take one more transaction

Redeeming a wrapped token can take a burn, a release, and sometimes a separate claim; knowing which step is pending helps prevent duplicate actions on the source chain.

Onchain Report NewsroomOct 16, 2026 · 23:54 UTC#60a52d3 min read

Redeeming a wrapped asset can take more than one transaction because the token must be removed from one chain and its backing asset released on another. A swap turns one token into another; redemption asks the system that issued the wrapped token to return the asset it represents. That extra step can feel like a delay, but it is often part of how the system tracks the request.

What happens when you redeem a wrapped token?

A redemption usually starts when you send the wrapped token to the contract or service that issued it. A contract is code that carries out rules on a blockchain. In many systems, the wrapped token is burned, meaning it is taken out of circulation, and a message or record is sent to the chain holding the backing asset.

The asset may then be released to your wallet. Some systems complete that release automatically. Others ask you to submit a separate claim transaction after the request is ready. The exact steps depend on the token and the route you used. Check the issuer’s instructions for the token’s contract address, supported chains, and redemption method before sending funds.

Why might a second transaction be needed?

Blockchains do not share one common record, so a system moving value between them needs a way to confirm that the first action happened. That confirmation can take time. A separate claim lets you collect the released asset once the destination chain has recorded the request. It may also be needed when the system requires you to pay the destination chain’s transaction fee yourself.

Pending status can mean different things: the first transaction may still be waiting for confirmation, the cross-chain message may still be processing, or the asset may be ready to claim. Look at the transaction status on the chain where it was submitted, then check the redemption page for any claim action. If a swap is pending instead, these steps for a pending quickswap swap explain what to check. Avoid submitting the same redemption again just because the balance has not changed yet.

What should you check before you redeem?

Confirm the destination chain and wallet address first. A correct token sent to the wrong chain or address may not arrive where you expect. Also check whether you need the destination chain’s native token to pay a claim fee. A wrapped token is not always redeemable through every wallet or swap screen; use the process listed by its issuer.

  • Verify the wrapped token and its issuing system.
  • Check the destination chain and receiving address.
  • Keep the transaction ID so you can track the request.
  • Wait for the status to update before retrying or claiming.

The practical rule is simple: treat redemption as a tracked process, not a single send. Find out whether your request is waiting, processing, or ready to claim, then take only the next step the issuer’s interface shows.
