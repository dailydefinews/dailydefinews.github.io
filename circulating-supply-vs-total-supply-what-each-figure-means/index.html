Circulating supply vs total supply: What each figure means

Circulating supply vs total supply in crypto: total counts minted tokens minus burns; circulating counts the share available to trade, leaving locked tokens out.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 24, 2026 · 07:06 UTC#2e6ae12 min read

Circulating supply is the part of a token’s total supply available to trade; total supply is every token currently in existence, including locked tokens. Locked team or treasury tokens exist, but cannot yet reach the market.

On Solana, a token’s mint account records its total supply. Circulating supply is estimated by data providers, which decide which holdings count as available. A rugcheck can also show the mint’s supply and whether its mint authority is still active.

Why can two sites show different figures?

Solana has one on-chain supply figure in the mint account, but no single on-chain circulating supply figure. Providers estimate circulation using their own methods, so Solscan and DexScreener can show different circulating figures for the same token.

CoinMarketCap’s supply methodology treats locked or reserved tokens as outside circulation. Providers may also assess which holdings are available to sell in different ways. That is why the circulating figure is an estimate rather than a field stored on Solana.

How to read the gap before buying

Say a project has minted 1,000,000 tokens, with 400,000 locked for the team. Total supply is 1,000,000. If the other 600,000 are available to trade, circulating supply is 600,000. The 400,000-token gap exists, but is not yet part of the market’s available supply.

That gap matters because locked tokens may reach the market later. Find out when they unlock before buying: an unlock can increase the supply available to trade. Total supply alone does not tell you when that will happen, so look for the project’s unlock schedule.

The Solana Token Program documentation describes the mint account’s supply and mint authority. If that authority remains active, its holder can mint more tokens and raise total supply. If tokens are burned, they leave total supply. So a fixed mint supply and a large locked allocation tell you different things: one limits new issuance, while the other points to existing tokens that may become tradable.

When reviewing a token, compare the provider’s circulating figure with the mint’s total supply, then look at unlock timing and Holder concentration—the share held by a few wallets. That gives you a clearer picture of current tradable supply and what could join it later.
