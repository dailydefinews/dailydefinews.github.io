JPMorgan, HSBC and StoneX complete digital gold trades

JPMorgan, HSBC and StoneX tested digital claims on London gold bars, settling trades and using the interests as collateral in a first for wholesale markets.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 25, 2026 · 14:40 UTC#54cb842 min read

JPMorgan, HSBC and StoneX have completed the first live trades using digital interests in physical gold in London, and used those interests as collateral. The World Gold Council’s announcement says the legally binding wholesale transactions tested a new way for institutions to transfer and use gold.

What did the banks trade?

They traded Pooled Gold Interests, or PGI: electronic records that give participants ownership interests in pooled physical gold. The gold bars were 400-ounce London Good Delivery bars, a standard for wholesale bullion. The interests represented fractional ownership, so participants could trade part of the pooled gold rather than move whole bars.

The council says the bars entered its Wholesale Digital Gold system, where the PGI were created. After the trades settled, the gold was withdrawn from the system, completing the full process. It did not say how many bars or how much gold changed hands.

How could the interests be used?

The institutions also used PGI as collateral, meaning they pledged the gold interests to support financial transactions. That gives gold another potential role in wholesale markets: it could be transferred and pledged electronically while remaining in the gold system during the transaction.

The council says the system is designed to complement existing ways of settling gold, including allocated gold, where specific bars are assigned to an owner, and unallocated gold, where a customer has a claim on a quantity of gold rather than particular bars.

Who built the system, and what comes next?

The World Gold Council developed the initiative. ICE Benchmark Administration operated the pilot, and Baton Systems provided the technology, according to the council. The initiative was first launched in September 2025 and remains at pilot stage.

CoinDesk’s report on the trial says the institutions did not disclose the value of gold traded. It also reports that rules for governing the system and a timetable for wider availability have not been finalized. The council says the pilot is intended to make gold easier to trade and use in financial markets while preserving ownership of physical gold.
