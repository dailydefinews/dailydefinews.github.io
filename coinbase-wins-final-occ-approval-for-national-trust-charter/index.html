Coinbase wins final OCC approval for national trust charter

The OCC has made Coinbase’s national trust charter effective, putting its planned institutional crypto custody business under a federal framework.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 9, 2026 · 22:00 UTC#df9fb12 min read

Coinbase has received final approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) to operate a national trust company, bringing its planned institutional crypto custody business under a federal framework. The company’s announcement says the OCC approved the application on Friday, Nov. 6. The regulator’s filing record lists the charter as effective that day.

The approval completes a process that began with conditional approval in April. A national trust charter lets the company provide custody—holding assets on behalf of clients—as a federally supervised trust business. It does not give Coinbase a full-service bank license.

What can Coinbase’s trust company do?

Coinbase National Trust Company plans to hold digital assets for clients, mainly institutions, in a fiduciary role: it must act in the clients’ interests when managing those assets. The OCC’s April decision describes related services that let custody clients accept, hold and transfer cash and digital assets in their custody accounts.

The OCC’s charter record identifies the entity as Coinbase National Trust Company, based in New York. The regulator approved its application on April 2 and marked it effective Nov. 6. That final step followed the preliminary approval, which required Coinbase to meet pre-opening conditions before beginning business.

Does this make Coinbase a regular bank?

No. The charter is limited to trust company work and related activities. The OCC’s April decision says the bank must stay within that scope. Coinbase National Trust Company cannot use this charter to take ordinary deposits or make loans like a full-service bank.

The new federal charter also does not mean all Coinbase custody operations have already moved to the new entity. In its April decision, the OCC said Coinbase planned to move its existing custody business from its New York-chartered trust company over a three-year period. Coinbase’s announcement describes the federal approval as a step toward expanding its custody business under a federal framework.

For institutional clients, the change puts the planned trust company’s custody work under direct OCC oversight. Coinbase said it will continue working with New York’s financial regulator, while bringing the national trust company’s activities into a consistent federal framework.
