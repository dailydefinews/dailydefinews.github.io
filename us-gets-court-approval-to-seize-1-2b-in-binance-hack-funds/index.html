US gets court approval to seize $1.2B in Binance hack funds

The Justice Department has court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack funds, while efforts to trace and recover the rest continue.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 17, 2026 · 23:49 UTC#86f3332 min read

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a major step in a recovery effort that has already frozen more than $2.7 billion. The Justice Department announced the action on Nov. 12, saying it is working with private companies and law enforcement to trace and stop the funds.

The FBI had attributed the Binance theft to TraderTraitor, a group of North Korean military members, and said it happened on or around Oct. 29. The Associated Press report said the theft involved about $6.3 billion in crypto. The FBI’s attribution is also set out in a FBI alert.

Court approval covers part of the frozen funds

The Justice Department said government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets so far. The court-authorized amount—more than $1.2 billion—is part of that total. The department said the order allows the US to seize or restrain the assets; it did not say the money has been returned to Binance or its customers.

Seizing or restraining crypto can stop its movement while authorities pursue the case. But the department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are still underway. It said TraderTraitor actors continue to move the crypto through bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchains, while mixers can make a transaction trail harder to follow.

The FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation have urged crypto businesses to block transactions linked to addresses the group is using to launder the stolen assets. That warning followed an FBI alert naming addresses holding, or previously holding, funds from the theft.

The $1.2 billion action was one part of a broader Justice Department announcement on North Korean crypto theft. The department also said it had seized cloud infrastructure used in a separate scheme called FaceStealer, which it linked to more than $400 million in crypto theft over 18 months. That operation is separate from the Binance case.

The next step in the Binance case is continued tracing and legal action over the assets. The Justice Department has not announced a timeline for forfeiture or any return of funds.
