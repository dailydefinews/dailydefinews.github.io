How Long Does a Polygon PoS Withdrawal Stay Pending?

A Polygon PoS withdrawal often takes a few hours because it waits for an Ethereum checkpoint, then needs a separate claim transaction to finish.

Onchain Report NewsroomOct 4, 2026#54e57e3 min read

A Polygon PoS withdrawal to Ethereum often takes a few hours, and it may stay pending until you complete a second transaction. The wait is part of the bridge process: it gives validators time to record your withdrawal on Ethereum before the funds can be claimed.

The Polygon Bridge uses a two-step withdrawal. First, your tokens are burned on Polygon. Then a checkpoint—a record of Polygon activity submitted to Ethereum—makes that withdrawal ready to claim. For more on how the route works and what to check before choosing it, see this guide to choosing a Polygon Bridge route.

Why is my Polygon withdrawal still pending?

It may be waiting for a checkpoint, or it may be waiting for your claim transaction. These are separate stages, and the bridge interface may show both as part of one withdrawal.

After the burn succeeds on Polygon, the tokens leave your Polygon wallet. They are not yet in your Ethereum wallet. The bridge must wait for validators to include the relevant Polygon activity in a checkpoint on Ethereum. Once that happens, you can submit the claim transaction on Ethereum to release the tokens.

That wait is usually measured in hours, but it has no guaranteed finish time. Checkpoint timing can vary, and Ethereum congestion can slow the final claim. A “pending” label by itself does not tell you which stage is holding things up.

What should I check while I wait?

Check the Polygon transaction first, then look at the withdrawal’s status in the bridge interface. This helps you tell a slow bridge step from a transaction that never confirmed.

  • If the burn transaction is still pending in your wallet, check its status on a Polygon block explorer.
  • If the burn confirmed and the bridge says it is waiting for a checkpoint, give that stage time to complete.
  • If the checkpoint arrived and the interface says action is required, switch to Ethereum and submit the claim.
  • If the Ethereum claim is pending, check its transaction status; network congestion or a low gas fee can delay confirmation.

The claim is a separate Ethereum transaction, so you need ETH in the same wallet to pay its gas fee. If the bridge offers a retry for a stuck transaction, check that the original transaction’s status before trying again.

Can I cancel or leave the withdrawal pending?

Once the Polygon burn has been initiated, the withdrawal cannot be cancelled. Polygon Support says there is no time limit to complete the pending withdrawal, so you can return to claim it later. Keep access to the same wallet and use the bridge’s transaction status to resume.

For most readers, the practical takeaway is simple: a few hours of waiting can be normal, but the process is not finished until the Ethereum claim confirms. Check which step is pending before taking action.
