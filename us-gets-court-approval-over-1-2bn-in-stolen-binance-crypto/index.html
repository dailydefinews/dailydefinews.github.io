US gets court approval over $1.2bn in stolen Binance crypto

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in crypto stolen from Binance, as efforts to track and recover the funds continue.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 16, 2026 · 08:01 UTC#cb76972 min read

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said on November 12. The move is part of a wider effort to recover funds from a theft the FBI attributes to North Korean hackers.

In its announcement, the Justice Department said the October 29 theft involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. It identified the hackers as members of the North Korean military group the FBI calls TraderTraitor.

The $1.2 billion has not necessarily been returned to Binance or its users. The department says the court has authorized its seizure or restraint. That is a legal step that can let authorities take control of assets or stop them moving while they pursue forfeiture.

More than $2.7 billion has been frozen

Since the theft, US agencies and private companies have worked with virtual asset service providers—companies that handle crypto transfers—to track and block the stolen funds. The Justice Department said those joint efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion so far. The court-authorized amount is part of that total.

The FBI had also issued a cyber alert the previous week, asking crypto service providers to block transactions involving wallet addresses used to move the stolen Binance assets. The department says investigators are still tracing the funds as the hackers move them through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchains; mixers can make the path of funds harder to follow.

The Associated Press reported that the FBI described more than $1.2 billion as seized. Its account of the Justice Department announcement says the government has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain that amount. The distinction matters: the announcement does not say the funds have been returned to their owners.

Recovery work continues

The Justice Department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen assets are ongoing. Forfeiture is the legal process for transferring property to the government. The AP reported that authorities typically file a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The next confirmed step is continued work to track and restrain the assets. The Justice Department has not announced a final forfeiture decision or a date for any return to Binance or its users.
