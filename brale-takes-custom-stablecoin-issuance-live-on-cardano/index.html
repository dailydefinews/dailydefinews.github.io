Brale takes custom stablecoin issuance live on Cardano

Brale has opened Cardano to custom USD stablecoin issuance on mainnet and testnet, giving businesses a new route for payments, payouts and treasury use.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 27, 2026 · 00:32 UTC#7b1c522 min read

Brale’s stablecoin service is now live on Cardano, letting businesses issue their own regulated, dollar-backed tokens or use stablecoins for payments and treasury work. Brale announced the launch on Nov. 24, saying the service is available through its app and API on both mainnet and testnet. Brale’s launch announcement says the integration uses CIP-113, Cardano’s standard for programmable tokens.

What can businesses do with Brale on Cardano?

They can create a USD stablecoin under their own brand, or use stablecoins already supported by Brale. The service is designed for settlement, payouts, payroll and cross-border payments. Brale says it handles the supporting work: minting and redemption, custody, reserve management, compliance and connections to banks.

For a typical payment, a customer sends dollars to Brale from a bank account. Brale then issues the stablecoin on Cardano and sends it to the destination wallet. Businesses already using Brale can add Cardano through the existing integration, according to the company.

What does CIP-113 change about the tokens?

CIP-113 lets issuers set rules that apply when a token moves. The transfer happens only if scripts—code that checks the rules—approve it. Brale says this lets compliance controls sit with the token itself.

The rules can vary by token. Brale points to controls such as freezing or seizing funds, but that does not mean every token has those powers. The issuer chooses which policies to include.

Is the service available, and how broad is Brale’s network?

Yes. Businesses can use the service on Cardano’s mainnet, the live network, and testnet, a separate environment for testing. Cardano is Brale’s latest supported blockchain, bringing its network to more than 30 chains. Brale says stablecoins issued through its service can move across those networks.

The Cardano Foundation’s Nov. 25 community digest says the launch follows a partnership announced about a year earlier. Brale’s launch post did not name a business issuing a token at launch. The service is live, but the announcement does not establish how many firms will use it or how much activity it will bring to Cardano.
