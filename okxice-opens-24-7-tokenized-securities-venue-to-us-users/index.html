OKXICE opens 24/7 tokenized securities venue to US users

OKXICE opened its round-the-clock securities venue to eligible US customers, with 113 stocks, ETFs and ETPs set for phased rollout in the OKX app.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 24, 2026 · 04:38 UTC#eb78312 min read

OKXICE opened its tokenized securities venue to eligible US customers on Nov. 23, offering trading around the clock through the OKX app. The OKX launch announcement says 113 assets are confirmed for a phased rollout. That means the full lineup was not necessarily available at launch.

The venue is a joint venture between crypto exchange OKX and Intercontinental Exchange, the parent company of the New York Stock Exchange. OKX says trading began at noon Eastern time for eligible customers, subject to identity checks, onboarding and geographic limits.

What the 113 assets include

  • 113 securities: The confirmed list includes stocks, exchange-traded funds (ETFs) and exchange-traded products (ETPs). OKX says they will become available on a rolling basis.
  • Named issuers: The lineup includes Better.com, BitGo, Circle, Coinbase, Direxion, eToro, Fidelity Investments, Robinhood, Securitize, Strategy, Strive and Vida Global.
  • 67 issuer objections: The Block reported that 67 companies filed objections to tokenized versions of their shares being offered. Those include Apple, Nvidia and Amazon. The figure gives context to the confirmed lineup: not every major listed company is participating.

How trading works

Each token represents a security entitlement backed one-to-one by an underlying share, according to OKX. The entitlement includes applicable shareholder rights and economic benefits, such as dividends, voting rights and corporate actions.

Trades use automated market maker pools on Uniswap v4, a system that matches trades through shared pools of assets, on X Layer, OKX’s Ethereum network. Securitize provides the tokenization infrastructure. Smart contracts restrict trading and liquidity provision to eligible wallets. Customers keep the securities in self-custodial wallets, meaning they control their own keys.

The venue operates under a temporary SEC exemption, but OKX says it is not a registered exchange or broker-dealer and is not approved by the SEC. The New York Stock Exchange does not operate or oversee it. Tokenized securities may also trade at prices that differ from the underlying shares, especially outside regular market hours.

For crypto users, the launch brings selected traditional securities into an onchain trading system that runs beyond normal stock-market hours. Access remains limited to eligible US customers, and the 113 assets are scheduled to arrive in stages.
