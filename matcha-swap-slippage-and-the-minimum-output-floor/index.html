Matcha swap slippage and the minimum-output floor

What does slippage mean on Matcha swap? It is the gap between a quote and execution; tolerance sets the minimum output you accept before the swap fails.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 18, 2026 · 22:01 UTC#9ced103 min read

Slippage on a Matcha swap is the change between the quoted exchange rate and the rate available when the transaction executes. Prices and available liquidity can shift while it waits, so the final output may differ from the estimate.

The quote and the minimum received are two separate numbers. Slippage tolerance sets how far the output can fall below the quote before the swap fails; it does not improve the exchange rate. That difference matters when considering Matcha swap slippage tolerance and MEV protection, because tolerance sets an execution floor while MEV protection addresses a different source of swap loss.

How does slippage tolerance set the minimum output?

Slippage tolerance sets the lowest output you agree to accept, based on the quoted amount. For an exact-input swap quoting 100 tokens with 1% tolerance, the minimum received is 99 tokens.

That 99-token figure is the floor, not a prediction. The quote is an estimate, and execution uses the rate available when the transaction is processed. If the available rate produces 99 tokens or more, the swap can proceed; if it falls below 99, the swap fails.

Say you are swapping a fixed amount of one token and the quote shows 100 of another. Reading the minimum received alongside it tells you the least output you have agreed to accept. The 1% setting does not promise 99 tokens; it sets the point below which the transaction should not complete.

Why can a swap fail because of slippage?

A swap can fail because the output available at processing time has fallen below its minimum. The market price may move while the transaction waits, or the swap itself may move the price when liquidity is limited.

A tighter tolerance means less acceptable loss against the quote, but it also leaves less room for price movement. In a changing market, even a small shift can push the output below the minimum and make the swap fail. A wider tolerance allows a larger drop before failure, while accepting a lower possible output.

In a constructed case, imagine a beginner swapping into a token with limited liquidity. They want to avoid accepting much less than the quote, so they compare the quoted output with the minimum received before deciding whether the floor suits them. That makes the setting useful: it turns a percentage into a concrete amount they can judge.

Related questions

What is minimum received on a token swap?

Minimum received is the lowest output allowed by the tolerance chosen for that quote. It is calculated from the quoted output and that tolerance.

Why can a swap fail because of slippage?

It fails when the output available at execution drops below the agreed minimum. Check both the quote and minimum received before approving the swap to see the floor you are accepting.
