US court authorizes seizure of $1.2B in Binance crypto

US says a court authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion tied to the Binance theft, as efforts to recover the crypto continue.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 19, 2026 · 22:09 UTC#7d0a9f2 min read

The US Justice Department says a court authorized the seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The move is part of a wider effort to recover funds from the exchange’s October theft. In its announcement on Nov. 12, the department said the money was part of a larger pool already frozen. The authorization does not mean the department has confirmed that all of it has been transferred to government control.

The Justice Department said US agencies and private firms have frozen more than $2.7 billion connected to the theft. The newly authorized amount is part of that total. The department did not say how much of the $1.2 billion is now in government custody.

FBI links the October theft to North Korea

The department said members of North Korea’s military stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or around Oct. 29. The FBI calls the activity “TraderTraitor.” The FBI has attributed the theft to North Korea, and the Justice Department says efforts to trace and recover the funds are still underway.

Authorities and their partners have worked with virtual asset service providers — businesses that handle crypto transactions — to freeze the stolen assets. The department said the attackers continue to move funds through crypto bridges, which move assets between blockchains, as well as mixers and exchanges. Mixers are services that make it harder to trace where crypto came from.

On Oct. 31, the FBI urged crypto services and other industry groups to block transactions linked to wallet addresses associated with TraderTraitor. The FBI said the stolen assets were being converted mainly into Ether, and warned they could be moved through decentralized finance and instant-swap services.

Forfeiture could decide who gets the funds

The Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of the frozen assets. The department says tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture work continues. Forfeiture is the legal process that can transfer property to the government.

The Associated Press reported that in such cases the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who decides who has a valid claim to the recovered funds. The Justice Department has not announced the outcome of that process for the Binance assets.
