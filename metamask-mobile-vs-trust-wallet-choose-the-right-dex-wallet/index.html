MetaMask Mobile vs Trust Wallet: choose the right DEX wallet

MetaMask Mobile vs Trust Wallet for DEX trading: choose MetaMask for EVM trades and desktop pairing, or Trust Wallet for holding assets across many chains.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 20, 2026 · 23:48 UTC#7bd7623 min read

Choose MetaMask Mobile for EVM trading that you also do on desktop; choose Trust Wallet if you hold assets across many chains. Both are self-custodial wallets that can connect to decentralized exchanges, so your chains and trading routine matter more than the wallet brand.

Which wallet fits your chains and desktop routine?

MetaMask Mobile suits traders who mainly use Ethereum and other EVM networks, while Trust Wallet covers more non-EVM chains in one app. EVM networks use technology compatible with Ethereum’s virtual machine. Trust Wallet supports a broad mix that includes networks such as Bitcoin and Solana.

For a DEX such as shiba swap, the wallet connects to the exchange and approves transactions; the DEX handles the trade. That distinction helps when comparing the exchange’s pool fee with a wallet’s separate swap service.

MetaMask Mobile pairs naturally with MetaMask’s desktop extension: importing the same recovery phrase gives access to the wallet on both. Trust Wallet also has a browser extension, but its main advantage is keeping assets from many networks in one app. MetaMask is built by Consensys; Trust Wallet is owned by Binance.

Both use BIP-39 recovery phrases, so you can import a phrase from one wallet into the other. Importing gives access to the wallet; it does not move funds between blockchains. Keep the phrase private, since anyone with it can control the wallet.

For Shibarium, either wallet can add a custom network. Confirm the chain ID against a trusted source before trading, so you connect to the network you intend to use.

How should you compare swap costs and DEX access?

Compare the built-in swap quote with the cost of trading directly on the DEX. A wallet’s in-app swap can route a trade through an aggregator, which searches across liquidity sources and may include a provider or wallet fee in the quote. MetaMask lists a 0.875% fee for its swaps. Trust Wallet says it adds no swap service fee, though its third-party providers may charge fees.

Trading directly through the DEX avoids the wallet’s built-in swap service. You pay the DEX pool fee and the network gas fee for the transaction. For frequent trades, those costs are easier to compare in the DEX’s own quote than in a wallet swap quote that may bundle in a provider charge.

On a phone, open shiba swap in a wallet’s DApp browser or connect through WalletConnect, then review the transaction in the wallet before approving it. MetaMask has a built-in mobile browser. Trust Wallet also supports DApp access, with WalletConnect as another way to connect from a mobile browser. The exact steps can vary by phone and app version.

Choose MetaMask Mobile if you trade EVM DEXes on desktop as well and want the same wallet there. Choose Trust Wallet if you hold assets across many non-EVM chains and want them together in one app.
