When a Manta Bridge Approval Can Save You a Transaction

A Manta Bridge deposit may need an ERC-20 approval first, but a sufficient existing allowance can remove that extra wallet transaction on later transfers.

Onchain Report NewsroomOct 23, 2026 · 06:18 UTC#fa4f8c3 min read

You can skip the extra ERC-20 approval transaction on a Manta Bridge deposit if your wallet has already approved enough tokens for the bridge to use. The approval grants permission; a separate deposit transaction moves the tokens. Knowing which step you are signing can help you avoid an unnecessary transaction and its gas fee.

What does an ERC-20 approval do?

An ERC-20 approval gives a named contract permission to use some of your tokens. It does not send those tokens anywhere by itself. Think of it as granting the bridge a spending limit before asking it to move funds.

When you bridge a token from Ethereum to Manta Pacific, the bridge checks that permission before it can take the tokens. If the allowance is too low, the flow may ask you to approve more, then ask you to confirm the deposit. ETH transfers do not use ERC-20 allowances, so they do not need this token approval step.

If you want more detail on the waiting time for that step, read how long Manta Bridge approvals take. Approval and deposit are separate actions, so check which one your wallet is showing before you sign.

When can you skip the extra transaction?

You can skip a new approval when the bridge already has enough allowance for the token and amount you plan to deposit. The allowance is tracked for a particular token and spending contract. A past approval for a different token or a different contract will not count.

Some users approve only the amount they plan to send. That limits the contract’s permission, but the deposit may use up that allowance, so another transfer can require another approval. A larger allowance can cover later deposits too, until the permitted amount is spent or the permission is changed.

Should you approve more than one deposit?

For most readers, approving the amount they expect to bridge is the simpler trade-off. A broader allowance can save future approval transactions, but it gives the bridge permission to spend more of that token. The choice depends on whether you value fewer wallet prompts more than a tighter spending limit.

  • Check the token name and network in your wallet.
  • Review the allowance amount and the contract named as spender.
  • Keep enough ETH on Ethereum to pay gas for approval or deposit transactions.
  • Do not assume an approval completed the bridge; confirm the deposit separately.

What should you check before signing?

Read the wallet prompt and confirm whether it is asking for approval or a deposit. If the allowance is already sufficient, the bridge should be able to proceed to the deposit without another approval. If it asks for a new approval, check the amount and spender before you accept.

The useful rule is simple: approval sets the permission, and deposit uses it. You can avoid a repeat approval when enough permission remains, but you cannot use an approval in place of the actual bridge transaction.
