Rebasing tokens vs share-based vault tokens: where yield shows

Rebasing tokens vs share-based vault tokens difference: rebasing raises your balance; share tokens keep it steady while each share claims more assets.

Onchain Report NewsroomNov 28, 2026 · 03:41 UTC#2882343 min read

Rebasing tokens show earned yield as a rising balance, while share-based vault tokens keep your balance steady and increase the assets each share can redeem.

  • Rebasing: your token count rises.
  • Share-based: your token count stays fixed as its value rises.
  • Both record a claim on the same kind of yield in different ways.

Where does the yield show?

With a rebasing token, the number of tokens in your wallet grows as yield accrues. stETH works this way: its balance changes as its share of staked ETH and rewards changes. Aave aTokens also use rebasing balances to reflect deposits and accrued interest.

With a share-based token, your token count stays the same. Instead, each share represents more underlying assets over time. ERC-4626, a standard for tokenized vaults, uses convertToAssets to show how many assets a given number of shares can redeem for.

wstETH is Lido’s non-rebasing wrapper for stETH: its balance stays fixed while its value tracks more stETH. mooTokens in the Beefy app are also share-based. The Beefy app is a concrete example of this model because its supported strategies automatically compound rewards, increasing the assets represented by each mooToken.

Which model fits your use?

Where yield shows: Rebasing suits holders who want their token balance to reflect accrued yield directly. Share-based tokens suit holders who prefer a steady token count and a rising value per share. The yield is accounted for in two places: the rebasing balance or the share’s exchange value.

DeFi composability: Rebasing can trip up contracts that cache a token balance and assume it stays constant. Wrappers such as wstETH offer a fixed balance for integrations that work more easily with standard token amounts. Share-based vault tokens also keep token counts fixed, while accounting for yield through the assets each share can redeem for. The Beefy app uses mooTokens to represent shares in its vaults.

Transfer precision: Lido documents a 1–2 wei shortfall on stETH transfers because balances are computed from shares. Small differences can arise when the share amount transferred is converted back into a displayed balance. Share-based tokens move exact share counts, so the transferred number of shares is precise even as the asset value per share changes.

Choose rebasing tokens if you want yield to appear as a larger balance and your integrations handle rebases. Choose share-based tokens if you want a steady balance, precise share transfers, or easier use across bridges and other protocols.
