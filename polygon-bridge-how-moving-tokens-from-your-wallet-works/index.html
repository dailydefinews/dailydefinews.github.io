Polygon Bridge: How Moving Tokens From Your Wallet Works

Polygon Bridge moves assets between Ethereum and Polygon through contract transactions, with different steps for deposits and withdrawals and fees on both networks.

Onchain Report NewsroomOct 21, 2026 · 19:01 UTC#c090473 min read

Polygon Bridge moves supported assets between Ethereum and Polygon by having your wallet send transactions to bridge contracts on each network. It lets you use an asset on Polygon without sending it through a person or a central exchange. Deposits and withdrawals follow different steps, and each needs gas, the fee paid to process a transaction.

What does Polygon Bridge do?

A bridge records a transfer on one network and makes the matching asset available on another. For a common Ethereum-to-Polygon PoS transfer, the bridge locks the tokens on Ethereum and makes a corresponding amount available on Polygon. The bridged token represents that value on the destination network; it is not the same token balance moving between two wallet addresses on one chain.

From your wallet, you choose the source and destination networks, select a supported token, enter an amount and approve the transaction. Some tokens require a separate approval transaction before the bridge can move them. The Polygon Bridge transaction then goes to a contract, which handles the cross-network accounting. Your wallet address can stay the same, but the networks and their transaction records are separate.

For example, moving a token from Ethereum to Polygon can make it available to use in Polygon apps. The bridge does not move the apps or your other wallet assets along with it.

How does a deposit or withdrawal work?

A deposit starts on Ethereum. You confirm any token approval, then sign the deposit transaction and pay Ethereum gas. Once the bridge processes it, the corresponding asset becomes available on Polygon. Check your wallet is set to Polygon to see the destination balance.

A withdrawal starts on Polygon. You send the asset to the bridge, which burns or locks the Polygon-side amount as part of the process. For the Polygon PoS bridge, the withdrawal must be recorded and verified before you can claim the released asset on Ethereum. That means a withdrawal can involve a wait and a separate claim transaction, with another Ethereum gas fee. Follow the transaction status and complete the claim when it is available.

What should you check before bridging?

Confirm the token and both network names before signing. Similar token names can refer to different contracts, and an asset may not be supported for the route you chose. Check that you have enough of each network’s gas token for the steps ahead. A transfer can take longer than a normal wallet payment because the bridge must process events across two chains.

  • Check the source network and destination network in your wallet.
  • Review the token, amount, and any approval request before signing.
  • Keep the transaction record so you can track a pending deposit or withdrawal.
  • For a withdrawal, check whether a separate claim is required and budget for its gas fee.

For most users, the official bridge route is the clearest option when moving supported assets between Ethereum and Polygon PoS. Its extra steps and fees are the trade-off for making the transfer through contracts rather than relying on a custodial service. A bridge transfer is a network operation, so check each transaction before you approve it.
